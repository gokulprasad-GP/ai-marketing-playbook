# 🧠 Strategy & Planning Prompts

Use these to sharpen thinking before campaigns and annual plans. Replace `[BRACKETS]` with your details.

---

### 1. Market sensing brief
```
You are a senior marketing strategist. Based on the information below, summarize the
key forces shaping the [CATEGORY] market in [REGION] over the next 12–24 months.
Organize your answer into: customer shifts, competitor moves, technology changes,
regulatory/economic factors, and implications for our brand.
Context: [PASTE NOTES, RESEARCH SUMMARIES OR ARTICLES]
End with the three strategic questions leadership should be discussing.
```

### 2. Positioning stress test
```
Here is our current brand positioning: [POSITIONING STATEMENT].
Act as three different critics: (1) a skeptical customer, (2) our toughest competitor,
(3) a CFO. For each, explain where this positioning is weak or unclear.
Then propose two sharper alternatives and explain the trade-offs.
```

### 3. Audience strategy
```
Our objective is [OBJECTIVE] for [PRODUCT/SERVICE].
Propose 4–6 audience segments based on needs and motivations, not just demographics.
For each: core need, key barrier, the message that would move them, the moments
and channels where they are most receptive, and how we would know it's working.
```

### 4. Annual plan pressure test
```
Below is a summary of our annual marketing plan: [PLAN SUMMARY].
Identify: (a) the biggest assumptions we are making, (b) the risks if those
assumptions are wrong, (c) what we should test early in the year to reduce risk,
and (d) where we may be over- or under-investing between brand and performance.
```

### 5. Scenario planning
```
Create three scenarios for the next 12 months in [CATEGORY]: optimistic, base case
and downturn. For each, describe what changes for our customers and what marketing
should do differently (budget, message, channel mix, pace). Present as a table.
```

### 6. Executive one-pager
```
Turn the following plan into a one-page summary for the executive team.
Structure: the opportunity, our strategy in one sentence, three priorities, what
success looks like, and decisions we need from leadership.
Keep it under 250 words and avoid marketing jargon. [PASTE PLAN]
```
